“Unlimited” is advertised, but a fair-use restriction is buried on another page

Your commercial pages advertise unlimited marketing sends, while a separate page qualifies usage with a fair-use or acceptable-use restriction. A customer deciding on price never sees the qualifier; a customer who hits it experiences it as a broken promise.

What your pricing says
Unlimited marketing sends (no specific plan)
“Pick Marketing Platform for $29/mo for up to 5,000 Active Users * , get unlimited marketing sends on paid plans. Add Bento Chat for $30/mo, or use Transactional Email on its own.”
Simple plans, no hidden fees.
What another page says
Usage is subject to a fair-use / acceptable-use restriction
“An Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) is a set of rules defined by an ESP or ISP that governs what type of content and behavior is allowed on their network.”
Email Marketing Glossary
Worth knowing: Fair-use clauses are standard practice. The issue is placement and wording, not the existence of the clause.

How to read these

Ambiguity — Both statements can be true, but a customer cannot tell what they get.
